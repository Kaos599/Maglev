An Android downloader for videos, links and torrents

A video saver, a download manager and a torrent client in one app, so you don't need three. Here is what each part does.

Every download in one list

Torrents, ordinary links and saved streams sit in the same list, each with its size, progress, speed and time left, such as 412 MB of 1.2 GB. A queue decides how many run at once. Pause, resume and retry survive a restart, and downloads keep going with the screen off.

  • One + button for a link you paste or type, a .torrent file, Search or the browser
  • Share a magnet, a .torrent file or a file link to Maglev from any app, or open .torrent files from your file manager
  • Play a video while it downloads, from its card or its screen
  • Open any download's folder in one tap, and share a finished file
  • On mobile data, the add screen shows the size before you start
  • Pause all, Resume all and Shut down from the notification
  • A live chip in the status bar on Android 16 and later
  • Unknown times show as unknown, never guessed

A browser that blocks ads and finds the files on a page

Open any page inside Maglev. Ads and trackers are blocked before they load. Play a video and Maglev lists it, together with the page's other files, in a tray you can filter by type. Tap one to download it with the page's cookies, so files behind a sign-in still work.

  • Over 42,000 blocked domains from StevenBlack/hosts, updatable in Settings
  • Popups you didn't tap, redirects into other apps and bad certificates are refused
  • App installers come with a warning

Streaming video, saved as one file

Many sites play video as hundreds of small pieces listed in a playlist: HLS (.m3u8) or DASH. Maglev reads the playlist, lets you pick the quality and audio language, downloads the pieces and joins them into one video file.

  • On-demand HLS and DASH, including HLS with standard AES-128 encryption
  • DRM-protected video and live streams are refused, and Maglev says why
  • Separate video and audio are joined without re-encoding

One search, many sources, one list

Type once and Maglev asks up to 40 built-in public torrent indexes at the same time, including ones you can switch off by category. Results that point to the same torrent are merged, so you see one row with every source that has it.

  • Filter results by category, and sort by seeders, size or date
  • 40 built-in sources; choose which answer each category
  • Add your own Jackett or Prowlarr indexer
  • Sources known for fake uploads carry a warning

Feeds that download new episodes for you

Follow a show from Search, or add any RSS feed. When a new item matches your rule, Maglev adds it to your downloads. Items that don't match are left alone.

  • Works with any RSS feed
  • Rules decide what gets added
  • Watch for a title: Maglev checks every 1, 6 or 24 hours (Wi-Fi only unless you allow mobile data) and tells you when something new turns up

An assistant that asks first (optional)

Ask in plain words, like "find the latest season of this show in 1080p" or "what's eating my storage?". It searches, compares results, checks size and free space, and shows what it wants to add. Nothing is added or changed until you confirm.

  • Runs on your own OpenRouter key, kept in the phone's secure storage
  • Can use free models
  • Keeps working when you switch tabs or leave the app
  • Chats are saved and come back after a restart
  • Turn it off and its tab disappears

You stay in control

Rules for when and how Maglev downloads, so it fits your data plan, your battery and your storage.

  • Download only on Wi-Fi, only over a VPN, or only while charging
  • Pause when the battery runs low
  • Speed limits for everything or for one download
  • A schedule for when downloads run or slow down
  • Pick the files in a torrent and mark one to download first
  • Pause all, Resume all and Shut down from the notification, and a Quick Settings tile
  • Files land in Download/Maglev, where any file manager finds them, or in any folder you choose
  • Move a torrent's files mid-download, and stream video before it finishes
  • Pauses before the phone runs out of space, keeping 500 MB to 1 GB free, and resumes when space comes back
  • Backup and restore in Settings: your settings, search sources, torrents and downloads in one file. API keys are never included
  • No analytics or ads SDKs

How it works

For the curious. Every engine build is compiled by GitHub Actions, which checks it for librqbit symbols and 16 KB page alignment.

Torrents

A modified copy of librqbit, written in Rust, with DHT, trackers, local peer discovery and web seeds.

Links and streams

The same Rust engine downloads ordinary links and stream pieces, keeps resume data next to the file, and checks free space before it starts.

Browser

Android's own WebView inside a native view that sees every request, so it can block ads and notice the video a player loads. No browser engine is bundled.

How to install Maglev

Maglev isn't on Google Play yet, so you install it from its GitHub page.

  1. 1

    Download the APK

    Open the latest release on your phone and tap the file ending in arm64-v8a.apk. Most phones from the last six years want this one. Pick armeabi-v7a for an older 32-bit phone, x86_64 for a Chromebook or emulator, or universal if you aren't sure.

  2. 2

    Allow the install

    Tap the downloaded file. Android warns about apps from outside the Play Store; that's normal for apps installed from GitHub. If it asks, tap Settings, turn on "Allow from this source", then go back and tap Install. On Android 7, turn on Unknown sources in Settings instead.

  3. 3

    Open Maglev

    Open the app and follow the short setup. Your downloads go to the Download/Maglev folder unless you pick another one.

Check it's the real Maglev

Official APKs are built by GitHub Actions from the public source and signed with one key. Its SHA-256 fingerprint is:

c8e15bd47fb39fb2248d8b501a677e61be564d52ba535e31a1608564e35df3c4

To check a file on a computer with the Android SDK, run apksigner verify --print-certs on the APK and compare the line "Signer #1 certificate SHA-256 digest" with the fingerprint above. If it doesn't match, don't install it.

Questions people ask

Is Maglev free?

Yes. Maglev is free to download and use, with no ads and nothing to buy. It is free software under the GNU General Public License v3.0, made by Harsh Dayal. The source code is public on GitHub, so anyone can check what the app does.

Does Maglev have ads?

No. Maglev has no ads and no ads SDK. Its built-in browser also blocks ads and trackers on the pages you open, using a list of over 42,000 ad and tracker domains that you can update or replace in Settings.

Is Maglev safe to install?

Yes, when you get it from the official GitHub Releases page. Maglev is open source, and its release APKs are built by GitHub Actions from the public code. Every official APK is signed with the same key, whose SHA-256 fingerprint is c8e15bd47fb39fb2248d8b501a677e61be564d52ba535e31a1608564e35df3c4. You can check it with apksigner before you install.

Is it legal to use Maglev?

Yes. Maglev is a download tool, like a browser or download manager; what matters is what you download and your local law. Maglev doesn't host or index any content: its search sends your query to public indexes run by third parties. Download only what you have the right to.

Does Maglev collect my data or use analytics?

No. Maglev has no analytics or ads SDKs. It contacts only what you use: the sites you open, the block-list addresses in Settings, the public sources you search, and OpenRouter when you use the assistant. Torrents also connect to trackers and other peers, as every torrent client does.

Why isn't Maglev on the Google Play Store?

Not yet. A Google Play build is planned. Until then, install the APK from GitHub Releases, or add Maglev to Obtainium, which installs new versions from GitHub as they come out.

Is Maglev available for iPhone?

No. Maglev is an Android app only. It runs on Android 7 and later, on phones, tablets, Chromebooks and emulators. There is no iPhone or iPad version.

Does Maglev work on Android 7?

Yes. Maglev runs on Android 7 and every later version. On Android 16 and later, download progress also shows as a live chip in the status bar.

How do I download videos from websites on Android?

Open the page in Maglev's browser and play the video. Maglev lists it in the Files tray with the page's other files. Tap it to download. Files that need the site's session still work, because Maglev uses the page's cookies for that download.

How do I save an HLS or m3u8 stream on Android?

Play the video in Maglev's browser, then tap the stream in the Files tray. Choose the quality and audio language and tap Start. Maglev downloads the pieces and joins them into one video file. It saves on-demand HLS and DASH streams, including HLS with standard AES-128 encryption.

What is a good m3u8 or HLS downloader for Android?

Maglev is one free, open source option. It finds HLS (.m3u8) and DASH streams while a page plays, lets you pick the quality and audio language, and joins the pieces into one video file. It handles on-demand streams, including AES-128 encrypted HLS, and refuses DRM-protected and live streams.

Which streams can't Maglev save?

DRM-protected streams, live streams and players that hide their video inside the page. That includes HLS that uses SAMPLE-AES, Netflix-style protected video and YouTube-style players. Maglev never gets around DRM, and when it can't save something, it tells you why.

Can Maglev download YouTube videos?

No. YouTube-style players hide their video inside the page in a way Maglev can't save, and Maglev says so when you try. The same goes for Netflix-style protected video and live streams.

Can Maglev save Instagram, TikTok or X videos?

Sometimes. Maglev has no special support for any one site. It saves video files and on-demand HLS or DASH streams that a page loads while you play them. Some players stream in a way Maglev can't save, and protected video never saves, so results vary by site and by video.

Is there an ad-free browser for Android that downloads videos?

Yes, Maglev's built-in browser is one. It blocks ads and trackers before they load, lists the videos and files a page plays or links to, and saves them with the page's cookies. Popups you didn't tap and redirects into other apps are refused.

What is a torrent, in plain words?

A torrent is a way to share a file between many people at once. Instead of downloading from one server, your phone gets small pieces from everyone who has the file and shares pieces back while it downloads. A magnet link or a .torrent file tells the app which file to look for.

Is Maglev a good LibreTorrent or Flud alternative?

It can be. Like LibreTorrent and Flud, Maglev downloads torrents and magnet links and follows RSS feeds. It also downloads ordinary links with resume, saves videos from web pages through an ad-free browser, and searches 40 built-in sources. The comparison page lists what each app's own page says.

Can Maglev open magnet links and .torrent files?

Yes. Open them from your browser, a chat app, the clipboard or a share. You see every file in the torrent before it starts, and you can pick only the ones you want.

Where can I find torrents to download?

In Maglev's Search tab. One search asks up to 40 built-in public torrent indexes, and merges results that point to the same torrent. Results from sources known for fake uploads carry a warning. You can also add your own Jackett or Prowlarr indexer.

Which APK file should I download?

The file ending in arm64-v8a.apk, if your phone is from the last six years. Pick armeabi-v7a for an older 32-bit phone, x86_64 for an emulator or a Chromebook, or universal if you aren't sure, because it runs on every device.

What permissions does Maglev need, and why?

Only what downloads need. Notifications show progress and finished downloads. A foreground service and wake lock keep downloads running with the screen off. Network state powers the Wi-Fi-only rule. Storage permission is asked only on Android 9 and older. Maglev can also ask to skip battery optimization and resume downloads after a restart.

Do I need root or a VPN to use Maglev?

No. Maglev needs no root and no VPN. If you use a VPN, you can tell Maglev to download only while the VPN is connected, and it pauses whenever the VPN drops.

Where does Maglev save downloaded files?

In the Download/Maglev folder on your phone, where any file manager can find them. When a download finishes you can open it, share it or open its folder right from the list.

Can I save downloads to a different folder?

Yes. You can choose any folder for your downloads. You can even move a torrent's files to another folder while it is still downloading.

Can I pause and resume downloads?

Yes. Pause, resume and retry work for torrents and for ordinary links, and they survive a restart. If a link has expired, Maglev asks for a fresh one and continues where it stopped.

Do downloads keep going in the background?

Yes. Downloads keep going with the screen off. If Android closes the app, Maglev comes back and carries on. The notification has Pause all, Resume all and Shut down buttons.

Can I download only on Wi-Fi?

Yes. You can set Maglev to download only on Wi-Fi, only when a VPN is on, or only while charging. You can also set speed limits and a schedule for when downloads run or slow down.

Does Maglev drain the battery?

It uses battery while it downloads, like any download app, and torrents also use it while they share with others. To keep it in check, Maglev can pause on low battery, download only while charging, and stop sharing a torrent once it reaches the ratio or time you set.

How do I get updates?

Add Maglev to Obtainium and new versions install as they come out. Or download the new APK from GitHub Releases and install it over the old one; your downloads and settings stay.

Does Maglev check for updates, and what does that send?

Once a day, and when you tap Check for updates, Maglev asks api.github.com for the latest release. GitHub sees your IP address and the app version. Turning off Check automatically in Settings → About stops the daily request. The update file downloads from GitHub's release file servers. By default Maglev downloads updates on Wi-Fi and asks before installing. Pick 'Tell me' to decide each time.

What does the AI assistant do, and do I need it?

You don't need it. The assistant is optional: it searches, compares results, checks size and free space, and explains problems, and it always waits for you to tap Confirm. It runs on your own OpenRouter key, kept in secure storage, and can use free models. Turn it off and its tab disappears.

Is Maglev open source?

Yes. Maglev is free software under the GNU General Public License v3.0: you can use, study, change and share it. The source code is on GitHub. The torrent engine is a modified copy of librqbit by Igor Katson, under the Apache License 2.0.

Is Maglev finished?

Maglev is stable, and it keeps growing: new search sources and features arrive in regular updates. If something goes wrong, please report it on GitHub.

Who makes Maglev?

How do I report a bug or ask for a feature?

Open an issue on GitHub. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, and include your Android version and phone model.
